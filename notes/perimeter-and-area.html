Perimeter and Area

Basics, fundamentals, formulas and examples. This page follows the chapter focus in the provided Grade 6 PDF.

1. What is area?

Area is the amount of region enclosed by a closed figure. It tells us how much surface is covered inside a shape.

Area is generally measured in square units.

Area of a rectangle = length × breadth
Area of a square = side × side = side²

2. Units of area

  • Square centimetre (cm²)
  • Square metre (m²)
  • Square unit (sq unit)

Remember: perimeter uses length units (cm, m); area uses square units (cm², m²).

3. Worked example — rectangle

A floor is 5 m long and 4 m wide. Find its area.

Area = length × breadth = 5 × 4 = 20 m².

4. Worked example — square

A square carpet has side 3 m. Find its area.

Area = side × side = 3 × 3 = 9 m².

5. Finding the remaining area

A floor measures 5 m × 4 m. A square carpet of side 3 m covers part of it.

  1. Whole floor area = 5 × 4 = 20 m².
  2. Carpet area = 3 × 3 = 9 m².
  3. Uncovered area = 20 − 9 = 11 m².
Remaining area = total area − covered area

6. Area of a triangle

A diagonal can divide a rectangle into two equal-area triangles. The area of a triangle is half the area of a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height.

Area of a triangle = ½ × base × height

Example: base = 8 cm, height = 5 cm.

Area = ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm².

7. Estimating area using grid paper

  • Each full unit square counts as 1 square unit.
  • Ignore portions smaller than half a square.
  • If more than half a square is covered, count it as 1 square unit.
  • If exactly half a square is covered, count it as half a square unit.

For complex figures, split the shape into rectangles and triangles whose areas can be calculated, then combine them.

8. Quick formula revision

Rectangle: A = l × b
Square: A = s²
Triangle: A = ½ × b × h
Remaining area = total area − used area
