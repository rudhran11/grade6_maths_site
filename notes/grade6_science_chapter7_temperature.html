1. Important Definitions

Temperature Very important

A reliable measure of the hotness or coldness of a body.

Thermometer Very important

A device used to measure temperature.

Clinical thermometer

A thermometer used to measure human body temperature.

Laboratory thermometer

A thermometer used to measure the temperature of substances in laboratory experiments and for other purposes.

Air temperature

The temperature of the surrounding air, which helps us understand weather conditions.

2. Temperature Scales Very important

ScaleUnitSymbol
Celsiusdegree Celsius°C
Fahrenheitdegree Fahrenheit°F
KelvinkelvinK
  • The SI unit of temperature is kelvin (K).
  • The normal average human body temperature is 37.0 °C or 98.6 °F.
  • Do not write a degree symbol with kelvin: write 300 K, not 300° K.
Temperature in K = Temperature in °C + 273.15

Example: 27 °C = 27 + 273.15 = 300.15 K.

3. Types of Thermometers

Clinical thermometer

  • Measures human body temperature.
  • Digital thermometers show readings on a screen.
  • Non-contact infrared thermometers measure without touching the body.

Laboratory thermometer

  • Measures the temperature of liquids and other substances.
  • Typical textbook range: −10 °C to 110 °C.
  • The liquid column rises or falls when temperature changes.
  • Read it while it remains immersed in the substance.

Room thermometer

  • Gives an approximate idea of room temperature.
  • Weather reports include maximum and minimum air temperatures.

4. Precautions While Using Thermometers Very important

Clinical thermometer

  1. Wash your hands and clean the thermometer tip before and after use.
  2. Follow the instruction manual.
  3. Keep the digital display portion out of water while washing.
  4. Do not hold the thermometer by its tip.

Laboratory thermometer

  1. Handle it carefully because it may break.
  2. Do not hold it by the bulb.
  3. Keep it vertical; do not tilt it.
  4. The bulb must not touch the bottom or sides of the beaker.
  5. Read at eye level while it is immersed.

5. Finding the Smallest Division Very important

The smallest division is the smallest temperature change shown by one small interval on a thermometer scale.

Smallest division = Difference between two major marks ÷ Number of small divisions
Example: Between 0 °C and 10 °C there are 10 small divisions.
10 ÷ 10 = 1 °C per division.

Practice: There are 20 small divisions between 0 °C and 10 °C. What is the smallest division?

Show answer

10 ÷ 20 = 0.5 °C.

6. Activities and Key Ideas

Activity 7.1 — Feeling hot or cold

Hands placed first in warm and ice-cold water may feel the same tap water differently.

Conclusion: Our sense of touch is not always reliable for measuring temperature.

Activity 7.5 — Measuring warm water

Immerse the bulb without touching the container. Wait until the liquid column stops rising, then read the temperature while the thermometer remains immersed.

Activity 7.6 — Boiling water

Students may record slightly different readings if they do not measure correctly. The textbook notes that the temperature of boiling water remains constant while it is boiling.

Activity 7.7 — Air temperature

Record maximum and minimum air temperatures for 10 successive days and compare the readings.
